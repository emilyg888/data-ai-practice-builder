## Post Insight Summary
The post positions Semantic_Foundry as the foundational governed layer for fraud intelligence. It transforms existing fraud code, features, and controls into a reviewable semantic package so AI components only reason over approved entities, signals, and metrics.

## Business Context
- Industry: Banking and Financial Services
- Domain: Fraud Detection Data and AI Governance
- Stakeholders: Fraud strategy, data governance, model risk, data engineering

## Problem Statement
Fraud programs often rely on fragmented feature definitions and undocumented assumptions across teams. This leads to inconsistent model behavior, difficult audits, and weak control boundaries. A governed semantic contract is required to standardize fraud signals and model outputs.

## Proposed AI/Data Use Case
- Objective: Build a certified semantic contract for fraud entities, signals, and prediction metrics.
- Primary User: Fraud analytics lead and governance reviewer.
- Decision Type: Human-approved promotion of semantic assets.
- Frequency: Continuous updates with controlled release cycles.

## Inputs
- Structured data: Customer, account, transaction, alert, and case data.
- Existing artifacts: SQL logic, feature pipelines, model score outputs.
- Governance references: Policy controls, quality rules, lineage metadata.

## Outputs
- Semantic package: Business entities, semantic views, fraud signal definitions.
- Governance artifacts: Validation reports, policy conformity checks, lineage.
- AI-ready outputs: Approved prediction and evaluation metric definitions.

## Workflow
1. Ingest existing fraud logic and related data assets.
2. Normalize entities, metrics, and signal definitions into semantic models.
3. Validate schema, rules, and policy boundaries.
4. Produce a reviewable certification package.
5. Publish approved semantic assets to downstream AI systems.

## Success Metrics
- Business KPI: Faster rollout of reliable fraud analytics assets.
- Model KPI: Reduced semantic drift across model versions.
- Operational KPI: Fewer governance exceptions during audits.

## Risks and Controls
- Definition drift risk: Versioned semantic contracts and release gates.
- Data quality risk: Deterministic validation and blocked promotion on failure.
- Compliance risk: Formal mapping from controls to semantic artifacts.
- Ownership risk: Explicit reviewer sign-off before production use.

## MVP Scope
- In scope: One fraud domain contract covering entities, signals, and outputs.
- Out of scope: Enterprise-wide harmonization across all risk domains.
- Timeline: 5-7 weeks for initial governed package.
